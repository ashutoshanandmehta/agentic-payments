Three Ways to Pay Over HTTP

P3P, x402 and MPP all revive the same dormant status code to let software pay for things. Here is what each one actually does on the wire — and the one question none of them answers.

They are more alike than the branding suggests. All three are challenge–response over HTTP 402: ask for something, get told the price, pay, ask again with proof. What separates them is what settles the money and who governs the spec.

P3P

Pine Labs UPI live in production

Launched 11 June 2026 · India · settles on UPI ReservePay (Single Block Multiple Debit) or One Time Mandate

The only one of the three that moves rupees. It stacks three things: UPI's own mandate rails underneath, an identity and delegation layer called Grantex in the middle, and an HTTP 402 interface on top.

Setup — happens once, with a human

# the merchant creates a mandate; the customer approves it by # scanning in their UPI app. this is the only human authentication. createMandate() → authorizationId

Every payment after that — no human

1 GET /api/gold-buy 2 402 Payment Required WWW-Authenticate: Payment <challenge> 3 client SDK decodes the challenge, builds a payment token 4 GET /api/gold-buy P3P-Credential: <payment token> X-Grantex-Token: <authorization grant> 5 server calls decidePayment(), captures against the mandate 6 200 OK Payment-Receipt: <receipt>

What Grantex enforces: agent identity, pre-approved spend limits, and an audit trail. Pine Labs' framing is "no guardrails, no payment." The customer can revoke the mandate at any time. Payment tokens are scoped to a specific resource, amount and expiry, so they cannot be replayed or redirected.

Who is live: Gullak, the digital-gold platform — an agent buys when gold hits a target price. Vijay Sales is running a proof of concept.

MPP

Stripe + Tempo IETF draft rail-agnostic

Launched 18 March 2026 · Machine Payments Protocol · settles on stablecoins, cards, or Bitcoin Lightning

The most carefully engineered of the three, and the only one on a standards track. It is formally an HTTP authentication scheme — proposed to the IETF as draft-httpauth-payment-00 — which means it slots in beside Basic and Bearer rather than inventing a parallel mechanism.

1 GET /v1/forecast 2 402 Payment Required WWW-Authenticate: Payment <challenge params> 3 client pays by whichever method it holds 4 GET /v1/forecast Authorization: Payment <credential> credential echoes the challenge + method-specific proof optional `source` field names the payer, in DID format 5 200 OK Payment-Receipt: base64url({status, method, ts, ref})

The design decision that matters: the core scheme knows nothing about how any payment method works. Methods are defined as separate IETF specs. Cards, stablecoins on Tempo, Lightning — and anyone can define another one.

It also ships streaming payment sessions, so an agent making a thousand calls does not pay a thousand times. That is a direct answer to x402's cost problem.

x402

Coinbase → Linux Foundation stablecoins only

Released May 2025 · governance moved to the x402 Foundation under the Linux Foundation, 2 April 2026 · settles USDC on Base

The oldest and the most widely deployed, with 22 launch members including Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Google and AWS. It introduces a third party the other two do not have: a facilitator, which verifies the payment and pushes it on-chain so the agent never touches a blockchain transaction directly.

1 GET /api/search 2 402 Payment Required PAYMENT-REQUIRED: {price, asset, payTo, network, scheme} 3 client signs an EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization — a gasless authorization, not a transaction. the payee address is inside the signature, so it cannot be swapped later. 4 GET /api/search X-PAYMENT: <signed payload> 5 server → facilitator /verify (amount, payee, sig, nonce, expiry) 6 server → facilitator /settle → submitted on-chain, awaits confirm 7 200 OK X-PAYMENT-RESPONSE: <settlement ref>

Read the adoption numbers carefully. Roughly 167 million settled transactions, but only about $28,000 in real daily commerce volume, with around half the activity gamified rather than genuine trade. Early volume was inflated by memecoin farming. SECONDARY

Known weaknesses: the facilitator is centralised — most verification runs through Coinbase's, and few deployments implement the local fallback. Researchers have documented replay, front-running and grant-before-settle attacks across the SDKs, plus a signature-verification bypass disclosed in March 2026.

The shared gap

All three bind a payment to a resource and a price — this URL costs this much. None of them binds it to what the user agreed to buy. A scoped, signed, unreplayable token for ₹600 is still a token for ₹600, even when the basket the human approved came to ₹118. Every limit holds and the user is still overcharged.

So — can you use them?

P3P

Yes, and read it closely

It is the closest thing to your thesis that exists: Indian, on UPI, on ReservePay, shipping. Treat it as the system you are positioned against, not a competitor to ignore. Using it needs Pine Labs onboarding.

MPP

Yes, as a carrier

Because payment methods are separate IETF specs, "UPI" could be defined as one. That is a cleaner route to a UPI binding than inventing a protocol, and the IETF path gives it standing an internal design does not have.

x402

No, except as a contrast

Crypto-only settlement, irreversible, and 30% tax plus 1% TDS per transfer makes it unusable for Indian retail. Its value to you is as the clearest example of payment authorisation carrying no record of intent.

What this means for the thesis

Your order gate is orthogonal to all three. It does not replace a payment protocol; it sits above one and answers a question none of them asks. That is a stronger position than competing with them — and it now holds against a live Indian system rather than a hypothetical one.
