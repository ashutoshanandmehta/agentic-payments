Delegated payments for repetitive purchases

Machines that reorder on their own, the check nobody is running before they pay, and why the layer above the rail is the durable position.

01Summary

A machine that reorders on its own has to pay for things without anyone watching. Every payment rail now shipping for agents checks how much it may spend. None checks what it actually bought.

That difference has a price. A ₹2,000 mandate happily authorises a ₹600 charge against a basket the owner approved at ₹118. Every limit holds, and the customer is overcharged by ₹482 with no rule broken and no dispute code to file under.

We build the check and the receipt: a deterministic test that a payment matches the approved order, and one signed record binding the mandate, the order and both payment legs. It runs on UPI and on cards, and the device owner picks which funds each agent.

Position

Not a rail, not a wallet, not a registry. The control and evidence layer above whichever rail moves the money — which is also the only position that stays neutral in a dispute.

02What we are delegating

Repetitive purchases. A machine notices it needs something and buys it again — filters, power, reagents, water, consumables. Not one-off shopping and not discovery.

This scope matters because it makes the problem tractable. The owner cannot approve each purchase, but they can approve the pattern in advance: this item, this much, from these suppliers, this often. That advance approval is the thing a payment gets checked against.

Where our work starts and stops

Starts when the basket is finished and the agent asks to pay. Stops when the money has moved and the evidence is recorded.

Choosing the product, comparing prices and picking the supplier are upstream. They are somebody else's problem, and treating them as ours has been the main way this project has lost time.

03Pain points

Four parties are in pain, for different reasons, and only one of them will pay us.

The device maker — our customer

  • To let their product transact they need a payment licence, a mandate system, spend controls a customer will trust, and somewhere for disputes to land. In every market they sell into.
  • They will not become a payments company to sell more water filters.
  • Building it once per country is worse than buying it once.

The owner — our user, not our customer

  • Will not let a machine spend without visible limits and a way to stop it.
  • Has no recourse when the machine buys wrong. NPCI's UDIR dispute system has reason codes for failed, unauthorised and fraudulent transactions and none for "the machine bought the wrong thing." SECONDARY
  • Cannot see across rails. The UPI block sits in their bank app, the card token in the issuer's app, and the purchase history nowhere.

The rail and the PSP

  • UPI has no field for an agent. A ReservePay block authorises a merchant to debit, not an agent to spend. The rail cannot tell an agent debit from any other. PRIMARY
  • Authentication happens once, at block creation. Every debit after that is unauthenticated by the human. PRIMARY

The merchant

  • As of mid-2026 no card network has published a binding chargeback rule for a dispute an agent started, and the one specification that addresses it in writing assigns the loss to the merchant. SECONDARY
The pain that pays

The owner feels the problem, but the device maker carries the cost — in support tickets, refunds, and customers who unplug the feature. That is why the device maker is the buyer even though the owner is the user.

Protocols we adopt rather than invent

  • HTTP 402 challenge–response. P3P, Stripe/Tempo's MPP and Coinbase's x402 all use it. Speaking it costs nothing and buys compatibility. SECONDARY
  • AP2 mandate shape. Google's Agent Payments Protocol defines Intent and Cart mandates as signed credentials. We follow the shape; the encoding can be plain signed JSON until it needs not to be.
  • Mastercard Verifiable Intent. Open-sourced March 2026. Ties a card authorisation to the human mandate behind it. We carry it on the card leg rather than reinventing it. SECONDARY
  • MCP on the agent side for tool access. Not a payment protocol and not treated as one.
What we deliberately do not build

A payment protocol. A rail. A wallet. A token standard. Every one of those has an incumbent with a network, and none of them is the missing piece.

07Core features

1. Agent identity and enrolment

Each agent gets a cryptographic identity chained to a verified owner and to the device it runs on. One device, many agents; one owner, many devices. Killing one agent never touches the others.

2. Authority creation, per rail

Per-purchase cap, period cap, category scope, expiry. Written to a ReservePay block on UPI or an Agentic Token on cards, from one interface.

3. The order check the differentiator

Before money moves, two deterministic tests. Does the payment still match the order — amount, payee, freshness, internal consistency. Does the order fit inside the authority — cap, category, period, count, window.

Arithmetic only. No model in the approval path, because a bank cannot refuse a payment on a probability; it needs a reason it can put in a dispute file.

4. The cross-rail receipt

One signed record naming the mandate, the order, the UPI leg and the card leg, with the amount matching across all of them or the record is invalid. Neither rail keeps this link. It is what turns a dispute from an argument into a lookup.

5. Owner console

Purchases first, each with the agent's signed order beside it. Limits underneath. One revoke button that fans out to three actions and reports each: kill the card token, stop future funding, return anything already sitting on the card.

6. Reconciliation and refund

Three outcomes, not two. Definitely failed, refund now. Definitely succeeded, nothing to do. Unknown, reconcile — never refund on a timeout, or you pay twice.

Refunds release the mandate headroom they consumed. Without that, a run of failures silently exhausts the owner's monthly cap while their balance looks fine.

08Payment security

The principle

Enforcement lives at the credential, not inside the agent. If an agent is prompt-injected, the limit does not move — because the limit was never in the agent's custody.

Controls

  • Fail closed. Unverifiable signature, unreadable payload, mismatched reference — all refuse. A checker that approves when confused is worse than none, because it launders a failure into an approval.
  • Ed25519 signatures over canonically serialised records, so tampering after signing is detectable. Deliberately not a shared secret: if the owner and the agent shared one, the agent could forge the owner's consent.
  • Integer paise everywhere. No float touches a comparison. A payments system that rounds can be gamed by rounding.
  • Replay protection on payment references, so a retried request is not a second debit.
  • Float minimisation. Load the exact basket total, pay immediately, sweep on failure. On the card path there is no float at all.
  • Revocation fan-out across both rails plus the float, each step reported. A revoke that half worked is worse than one that failed loudly.
  • Signed, timestamped revocation record. A payment landing one minute after a revoke is a liability question, and without a record it is our log against the merchant's.
What we do not claim

We do not prevent prompt injection. It is an unsolved research problem and anyone selling prevention is overselling. What we do is make injection unprofitable: an injected agent still cannot produce a payment that matches an order the owner approved.

We describe injection handling as risk scoring, never as prevention. That distinction is worth holding in front of a security-literate room.

The structural argument

A rail that adjudicates disputes about its own payments is a party to those disputes. If P3P also decides whether the basket was right, Pine Labs is judging Pine Labs.

Card networks are not merchants for the same reason. Neutrality is not a marketing claim here, it is the reason the evidence is worth anything.

10Differentiators

One that is technical

The order check. Everyone checks the limit; nobody checks the purchase. It runs in 30 seconds as a demo: ₹2,000 mandate, ₹118 basket, ₹600 charge — allowed by everything shipping today, refused by us.

One that is structural

We are the only party that can be neutral, because we never move the money.

One that is architectural

Both rails, one layer. The gap is different on UPI and on cards, and a rail company cannot fill both because it owns one.

One that is earned

Four things we found by building it, none of which is written down anywhere else:

  1. Signatures do not stop overcharging. Agent-signs, merchant-signs and both-sign all approve a self-consistent inflated basket. A signature proves who wrote a record, not that it is true.
  2. Routing through an agent's card destroys merchant scoping. The mandate can only name the card, so "this shop only" silently becomes "anywhere".
  3. A failed, refunded purchase eats the cap permanently unless headroom is explicitly released. The owner is whole in cash and short on authority.
  4. Revoking is three actions. Everyone implements one.

69 tests across rails, ledger, reconciliation and the order check. Anyone can claim to be building a control layer; this is what running one looks like.

12Go to market

Sequence

  1. One pilot in a live-today segment. An EV charging operator, a water-tanker aggregator, a diagnostics company. One integration proves the thesis end to end.
  2. Confirm the no-licence path in writing with a PSP or sponsor bank. This is a milestone, not an assumption.
  3. Publish the threat model and the four findings. The standards conversation is open now and will not be in a year.
  4. Then the platform channel. One integration, many customers — but a platform buys a threat model and incident data, so it has to be earned with direct customers first.

Entry motion

Enter through the engineer who deployed the agents and feels the risk first. Get signed by the finance or product owner who carries the loss. Expect the security lead as the gate, and bring the threat model rather than the demo.

Trigger events worth hunting

An incident. A statutory audit. A pre-launch review. A bank asking "what controls do you have?" These convert far better than cold outbound, because the budget already exists that week.

The honest obstacle

This category has no budget line. We do not create one — we borrow an existing one: fraud and risk, or audit and compliance, or product warranty. Same money, already approved, new problem.

Why not interchange

UPI is zero-MDR, so there is no interchange to share on the UPI leg. We cannot be an interchange business in India. We price as software, and treat card interchange as upside rather than foundation.

How to defend the price

Price against the loss, not the software. The comparison that lands is cost as a fraction of the spend under control, set beside card interchange at 150–200 basis points. One prevented incident should cover many months of fee.

How to position in their room

Do not position against P3P. It is their product, it shipped first, and it works. Position as the layer that makes it worth more:

  • The check P3P does not run. Grantex verifies the agent and its limits. Nothing verifies the basket. Our demo shows a ₹600 charge passing every control against a ₹118 approved basket.
  • The card bridge they are already seeking. Our design carries a single control layer across UPI and cards, which is the extension they have said they are working towards.
  • Neutrality they structurally cannot have. A rail that judges disputes about its own payments is a party to them. We never move money, so our record is worth more in a dispute than one either side wrote.
  • Distribution. Their merchant and device relationships are the channel we would otherwise spend two years building.

Use the home advantage

SIIC is our own institute's incubator and the research sits under a named supervisor at IIT Kanpur. That buys a hearing for depth that a pure pitch would not get — so lead with the four findings rather than the market story. In this specific room, rigour is the differentiator that is cheapest for us to prove and hardest for anyone to fake.
