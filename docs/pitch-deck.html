01

Agent payment infrastructure

Payments for machines that buy

Your fridge can order groceries. Your charger can pay for power. Neither of them can hold a payments licence — and neither should have to.

Ashutosh Anand · IIT Kanpur · 20 August 2026

02

What changed

The rails for agent payments arrived this year. The safeguards did not.

March 2026

Mastercard open-sources Verifiable Intent — proof that a human authorised an agent, carried in the authorisation.

June 2026

Pine Labs ships P3P on UPI ReservePay. Agents pay after one upfront approval. Live with Gullak.

Every one of them checks how much an agent may spend. None checks what it actually bought.

03

The gap, in one transaction

A limit is not a check.

Your fridge is authorised for ₹2,000 a shop. You approved a basket worth ₹118. The agent asks to pay ₹600 to that same shop.

mandate ₹2,000 · basket ₹118 · charge ₹600

Every system shipping today ALLOWED15 checks, all passed
With our check REFUSEDamount_matches_order failed

Not a slide. python src/cli.py consent runs this both ways in our simulator.

04

Who we sell to

The device maker has a payments problem they cannot solve.

A water purifier company wants the filter reordered automatically. A charger operator wants the car to pay by itself. An appliance brand wants the fridge to restock.

To do that, each of them needs a payment licence, a mandate system, spend controls their customer trusts, and somewhere for disputes to go. In every country they sell in.

They will not become payments companies. They will buy this.

05

Two rails, opposite holes

A machine in India needs both. Nobody sits across both.

UPI

Has: mandates, spend caps, ReservePay blocks, near-zero cost, and the volume.

Missing: no field for an agent. A block authorises a merchant to debit, not an agent to spend. And UDIR has no dispute code for "the machine bought the wrong thing."

Cards

Has: agent identity inside the token, merchant scope enforced by the network, 120-day chargebacks, and a credit line.

Missing: the token knows the limit and the category. It never knows what was in the basket.

Different gap on each. The same layer fills both — and a rail company cannot copy that, because they only own one rail.

06

The two pathways

Same purchase, same check — two architectures the owner chooses between.

UPI

pre-funded · ReservePay
1Owner approves a block in their UPI app, with PIN
2We issue the agent an identity and limits — the rail has nowhere to put them
3Agent builds the basket
4We check the payment against the basket
5Funds move out of the owner's account onto the agent's card
6Card pays the merchant
7Anything unspent sweeps back

Money rests with the agent between 5 and 7. Holding it needs a PPI licence. Zero MDR, and the volume is here.

Cards

post-paid · Mastercard Agent Pay
1Owner enrols their card; biometric ID&V, passkey bound
2Issuer mints an Agentic Token via MDES — agent id, caps, categories, expiry
3Agent builds the basket
4We check the payment against the basket
5Token presented at checkout with a Verifiable Intent credential
6Network validates the scope, issuer authorises the real card
7Cardholder → merchant, directly

Money never rests anywhere. The token routes; it holds nothing. ~2% MDR, and disputes already exist.

07

What we actually supply

Three things on UPI. One thing on cards. The one is the same on both.

On UPI

we build all three
  • Agent identity — the rail has no field for it
  • Scope enforcement — a block cannot express "groceries only"
  • The order check — does the payment match the basket

On cards

the network already does two
  • Agent identity — in the Agentic Token
  • Scope enforcement — validated network-side
  • The order check — nobody does this

The order check is the constant. Everything else is scaffolding that varies by rail — and on cards, most of it is already built for us.

08

Which machines need us

Three conditions. All three, or the check is worth nothing.

The amount varies

If the machine always pays the same ₹499, a spend cap already covers it. Our check earns its place when the price is not known in advance.

Nobody is watching

No screen, no person, no confirmation step. If a human approves each purchase, the human is the check.

Wrong costs real money

A ₹40 mistake is a nuisance. A ₹4,000 mistake is a support ticket, a chargeback, and a customer who unplugs the device.

This is why fixed-price subscription reorder — printer ink at a set rate — is not our market. There is nothing to check. We look for machines that choose.

10

Why us

We built it, and it broke in four places nobody has written down.

01

Signatures do not stop overcharging. We tested agent-signs, merchant-signs and both-sign. A self-consistent inflated basket passes all three. A signature proves who wrote a record, not that it is true.

02

Routing through an agent's card destroys merchant scoping. The mandate can only name the card, so "this shop only" silently becomes "anywhere". We have the payment that proves it.

03

A failed, refunded purchase eats the cap forever. The customer gets their money back while the monthly limit stays spent. A run of failures exhausts it invisibly.

04

Switching an agent off is three actions. Kill the token, stop the funding, return the float. Everyone implements one and calls it revoked.

69 tests across the rails, the ledger, reconciliation and the order check. Anyone can claim they are building a control layer. This is what running one looks like.

11

Both, always

The device maker integrates once. Their customer picks the rail.

Not a phased rollout and not a hedge. Two funding methods, offered side by side, chosen per agent by the person who owns it.

Fund by UPI

  • Money leaves the account as it is spent
  • Zero cost per transaction
  • Right for small, routine, predictable spend — filters, groceries, top-ups

Fund by card

  • Credit line — the owner is not out of pocket
  • 120-day chargeback if it goes wrong
  • Right for larger or less predictable spend

The control layer is identical either way. Same limits, same order check, same app, same revoke button. The owner sees a choice about their money, not a choice about plumbing.

Worth knowing internally: the card path holds no funds, so it needs no PPI or PA licence — issuer and acquirer enablement instead. The UPI path needs capital. That shapes which one we can ship first, not which one we offer.

12

The objection you are about to make

"Why doesn't Pine Labs just add this?"

Because a rail that adjudicates disputes about its own payments is a party to those disputes.

P3P moves the money. If P3P also decides whether the basket was right, Pine Labs is judging Pine Labs. A neutral record is worth more in a dispute than one written by a party to it — which is exactly why card networks are not merchants.

The same argument holds for us in reverse. We never touch the rails, so we have nothing to defend.

13

Honest risks

Three things that could sink this.

Regulatory

Whether agent debits sit inside RBI's e-mandate exemption is unresolved. If they do not, every agent payment on UPI today is non-compliant.

Prior art

P3P's full spec is not public. If it already binds payments to an approved basket, our wedge narrows to the cross-rail record.

Liability

No card network has published a binding chargeback rule for a dispute an agent started. The one specification that addresses it assigns the loss to the merchant. The recourse this depends on is real but untested.
