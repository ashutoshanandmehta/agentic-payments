01

Agent payment infrastructure

Payments for machines that buy

Your fridge can order groceries. Your charger can pay for power. Neither of them can hold a payments licence — and neither should have to.

Ashutosh Anand · IIT Kanpur · 20 August 2026

02

What changed

The rails for agent payments arrived this year. The safeguards did not.

March 2026

Mastercard open-sources Verifiable Intent — proof that a human authorised an agent, carried in the authorisation.

June 2026

Pine Labs ships P3P on UPI ReservePay. Agents pay after one upfront approval. Live with Gullak.

Every one of them checks how much an agent may spend. None checks what it actually bought.

03

The gap, in one transaction

A limit is not a check.

Your fridge is authorised for ₹2,000 a shop. You approved a basket worth ₹118. The agent asks to pay ₹600 to that same shop.

04

Who we sell to

The device maker has a payments problem they cannot solve.

A water purifier company wants the filter reordered automatically. A charger operator wants the car to pay by itself. An appliance brand wants the fridge to restock.

To do that, each of them needs a payment licence, a mandate system, spend controls their customer trusts, and somewhere for disputes to go. In every country they sell in.

They will not become payments companies. They will buy this.

05

Two rails, opposite holes

A machine in India needs both. Nobody sits across both.

UPI

Has: mandates, spend caps, ReservePay blocks, near-zero cost, and the volume.

Missing: the rail has no field for an agent. A block authorises a merchant to debit, not an agent to spend. And UDIR has no dispute code for "the machine bought the wrong thing."

Cards

Has: agent identity in the token, merchant scope enforced by the network, and 120-day chargebacks.

Missing: the token knows the limit and the category. It never knows what was in the basket.

Different gap on each rail. The same layer fills both. That is the product, and a rail company cannot copy it — they only own one rail.

06

What we are

Not a rail. Not a wallet. The check and the receipt.

Before the money moves

Does this payment match what the owner approved? Amount, merchant, basket, freshness. Arithmetic only — no model in the approval path, because a bank cannot refuse a payment on a probability.

After the money moves

One signed record binding the mandate, the basket, and both payment legs. The thing a dispute needs and neither rail keeps.

The owner gets an app: what each machine bought, what it was told to buy, and one button that switches it off — killing the card token, stopping future funding, and returning anything unspent.

07

Why us

We built it, and it broke in four places nobody has written down.

01

Signatures do not stop overcharging. We tested agent-signs, merchant-signs and both-sign. A self-consistent inflated basket passes all three. A signature proves who wrote a record, not that it is true.

02

Routing through an agent's card destroys merchant scoping. The mandate can only name the card, so "this shop only" silently becomes "anywhere". We have the payment that proves it.

03

A failed, refunded purchase eats the cap forever. The customer gets their money back while the monthly limit stays spent. A run of failures exhausts it invisibly.

04

Switching an agent off is three actions. Kill the token, stop the funding, return the float. Everyone implements one and calls it revoked.

69 tests across the rails, the ledger, reconciliation and the order check. Anyone can claim they are building a control layer. This is what running one looks like.

08

Where we start

Machines that already buy, before machines that might.

Already an eligible ReservePay category. NPCI built the primitive for this shape.

Water purifiers

Filter reorder on a schedule. Real Indian install base today.

Vending & smart retail

Unattended by definition. Small tickets, high frequency.

Smart appliances

The flagship story. The install base is not here yet — we arrive before it does.

Same product. We sell where machines already transact, and the fridge is where it goes.

Segment sizing is not in this deck. Say so if asked — do not improvise a number.

09

The objection you are about to make

"Why doesn't Pine Labs just add this?"

Because a rail that adjudicates disputes about its own payments is a party to those disputes.

P3P moves the money. If P3P also decides whether the basket was right, Pine Labs is judging Pine Labs. A neutral record is worth more in a dispute than one written by a party to it — which is exactly why card networks are not merchants.

The same argument holds for us in reverse. We never touch the rails, so we have nothing to defend.

10

Honest risks

Three things that could sink this.

Regulatory

Whether agent debits sit inside RBI's e-mandate exemption is unresolved. If they do not, every agent payment on UPI today is non-compliant.

Prior art

P3P's full spec is not public. If it already binds payments to an approved basket, our wedge narrows to the cross-rail record.

Liability

No card network has published a binding chargeback rule for a dispute an agent started. The one specification that addresses it assigns the loss to the merchant. The recourse this depends on is real but untested.

Before we publish it. The four findings are the asset; we would rather they were attacked here than in production.

Your bank knows you. Nobody knows your appliances.
