Trusted Delegated Payments for AI Agents over UPI and Cards

Objective, methodology and results for the simulator. It tests whether an agent payment can be bound to what its owner agreed to. It tests whether anyone on either rail is in a position to check. It measures what each rail costs you.

01Objective

The work begins when an AI agent tries to pay. The cart is already full. Something else chose the products and picked the shop. The question is deliberately narrow.

Is this agent allowed to make this payment, and can I prove it afterwards?

Product search, price comparison and shop selection all happen before the boundary being studied. They are out of scope.

Why the work spans two rails

The system covers UPI and cards because the two are asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the whole reason a layer above them is needed.

UPI is cheap and everywhere in India. Since Circular 201-B it can register a device or a software profile as a delegate. But what it enforces is a limit. It carries no agent identifier on the transaction, no merchant scope once an agent is involved, and no notion of what was bought.

Cards are the opposite. They are expensive, and card networks spent two decades building infrastructure that assumes an adversary. A card carries an agent identifier, enforces merchant and category scope at the network, and has a dispute process with defined liability. What it costs is custody. The money moves onto a credential the agent holds before the merchant is ever paid.

So the owner picks which rail funds a given agent, and the trade is different on each. The simulator implements both so the trade can be measured instead of argued.

The gap as originally stated

UPI makes you name the shop when you create a payment authority. UPI Autopay is locked to one payee. UPI Reserve Pay blocks money for one named merchant. But an agent does not know which shop it is paying until the cart is finished. So the authority has to be created before anyone knows what it is authorising. Existing UPI cannot express that.

A second gap sits alongside it. A mandate says how much may be spent and with whom. An operator policy says what is permitted. Neither knows what the user agreed to buy. A ₹5,000 mandate for brewhouse@ybl will authorise a ₹600 payment to brewhouse@ybl even when the basket the human saw came to ₹118. Every limit is respected. The user is still overcharged.

02The finding that changed the objective

The protocol landscape was checked directly before building further. That check moved the contribution.

AP2 v0.2 already specifies the order binding PRIMARY

Read from the AP2 specification and its flows page on 24 August 2026.

  • Open mandates are user signed constraints. They are created before a cart exists, for autonomous execution. That is the authorise before the merchant is known case.
  • Closed mandates bind to one transaction. The spec says the Payment Mandate is bound to a particular Checkout using the cryptographic hash of the Checkout JWT.
  • The merchant verifies that the constraints in the open Checkout Mandate have been met.

Binding a payment to an agreed order is therefore not a novel mechanism. It is specified. It is open source under Apache 2.0. It is backed by Google and the FIDO Alliance.

A related correction. The project brief section III A described AP2 as defining Intent, Cart and Payment Mandates. That is v0.1 naming. v0.2 defines a Checkout Mandate and a Payment Mandate. Each has an Open and a Closed stage. Both points are logged as C6 in docs/research/corpus-corrections-2026-08.md.

What survives, and why it is stronger

AP2 autonomous authority carries no spending limit PRIMARY

IntentMandate in the AP2 Python SDK has exactly six fields. user_cart_confirmation_required, natural_language_description, merchants, skus, requires_refundability and intent_expiry. No amount. No cap. No budget. No currency.

The v0.2 open Checkout Mandate carries vct, constraints, cnf, iat and exp. Its constraint types are checkout.allowed_merchants and checkout.line_items. It constrains who and what. It does not constrain how much.

This matters because of a result the simulator already had. What refuses an inflated order is a per transaction ceiling close to the real basket size. The signature does not refuse it. AP2 autonomous flow has the shopping agent sign its own closed mandate. The spec says the Shopping Agent may sign it using its Agent Key instead of getting approval on a Trusted Surface. That is exactly the arrangement shown not to bound the loss.

Caveat before this is quoted UNVERIFIED

constraints is an extensible array. A budget constraint type could be defined later. There may also be constraint types beyond those documented on the page read. Phrase this as no budget constraint type is defined among those documented. Never phrase it as AP2 cannot express a budget. Check it again before submission.

The revised objective

Two questions AP2 does not answer. Both are specific to this rail.

  1. No enforcement party on UPI. AP2 places the binding check at the Merchant Payment Processor. UPI has no such role in the path. The switch carries no such field. So where does the check run on a rail with nobody to run it?
  2. No UPI binding. AP2 says nothing about Reserve Pay or UPI Circle.

The order gate stops being the novelty. It becomes the instrument that produces the finding.

03Methodology: architecture

The proposed architecture

An authorisation and evidence layer that sits above both rails. The agent proposes. The layer disposes. The rail settles. The agent can never move money itself.

Agentassembles the cart and asks to pay
→
Order checkdoes the payment match the order
→
Authority checkdoes the order fit what was granted
→
RailUPI block or card token
→
Receiptone signed record of what happened

The order check runs before the authority check on purpose. Can they afford it is a different question from did they agree to it. Only the second one notices a ₹600 charge against a ₹118 basket. Both checks are arithmetic. No language model sits in the approval path, because a bank cannot refuse a payment on a probability. It needs a reason it can put in a dispute file.

The six components and their status

ComponentWhat it doesStatus
1. Agent registrationA cryptographic identity linking each agent to a verified owner and a host device. Several agents may run on one device, and revoking one leaves the others alive.Built
2. Authority creationThe owner sets a per purchase ceiling, a period budget, a category scope and an expiry. The authority then binds to a rail, which may not be able to carry all of it. Two shapes are supported. An agreed total for a basket, and a rate with a ceiling for anything metered.Built
3. The order checkBefore settlement, arithmetic confirms the payment matches the order and the order sits inside the authority. For a metered purchase it reconciles the amount against a meter reading instead.Built
4. The cross rail receiptOne signed record binding the registration, the authority, the order and every leg of money that moved. The amounts have to agree, and a spend has to chain back to the load that funded it.Built
5. Owner controlCreate, edit, revoke and monitor authorities. One view of purchase history across both rails.Partial. The owner app and 23 REST routes exist. Agents and receipts are not exposed yet.
6. ReconciliationEvery payment ends in one of three states. Succeeded, failed, or unknown. The unknown state matters because payment networks lose certainty about outcomes.Partial. The three states and the sweep are built. The dispute path is not.

Component 4 is the one that makes the rest provable. Its purpose is narrow. The load leg and the spend leg are separate transactions on separate rails, and nothing in either ledger says this spend came from that load. The receipt is what carries that link into a dispute.

Component 6 is the remaining gap. The three outcome states and the sweep are built. What is not built is the dispute path, which is what happens after a payment ends in the unknown state. The receipt is the input that path needs, so it was the necessary prerequisite.

What had to be fixed first

Four of the six components existed in some form. Two did not. One blocking design fault had to be fixed before either of the missing two could mean anything.

The blocker: verification required the private key

PRIMARY consent.py:79 verified a signature by calling self._private.public_key(). The wiring in sim.py held keypairs where a switch would hold registered public keys.

That is tolerable for the order gate experiment. It only asks what a signature proves. It is fatal for registration and receipts. Their whole claim is that a verifier holding no private key can check the evidence. If checking a signature needs the secret that made it, then the only party who can check is the party who could have forged it.

Separation of who holds what

The agent holds its own private key. Each merchant holds its own. That is legitimate. An agent really does hold its own key. The policy engine receives a Resolver holding only public halves. Nothing that reaches a verdict holds a secret belonging to a party it is judging.

One distinction fell out of this and it is load bearing. A did:key resolves for anybody. The key is inside the identifier. So resolving one tells you a signature is genuinely theirs. It tells you nothing about whether they were allowed to sign it. An identifier authenticates. Only an issued credential authorises. The Resolver therefore separates resolve() from is_registered(). Gates fail closed on the stricter one.

04Methodology: protocols adopted

Nothing was invented where a specification exists. The order gate is the contribution. Everything it stands on is somebody else's document. A reviewer can check the parts that are not mine against their own sources.

NeedAdoptedStatus
Agent identifiersdid:key v0.9W3C CCG draft. Not a Recommendation.
Revocation statusBitstring Status List v1.0W3C Recommendation, 15 May 2025
Signed artifactsRFC 8037, EdDSA in JOSEProposed Standard, Jan 2017
Key bindingRFC 7800, the cnf claimProposed Standard, Apr 2016
Attestation shapeRFC 9711 EAT, roles from RFC 9334 RATSProposed Standard and Informational

Two consequences that had to be designed around

  • A did:key cannot be revoked. It is generative and has no registry. There is nothing to update or deactivate. Revocation therefore lives in a separate status credential and not in the identifier. Revoking agent 3 flips bit 3. Agents 1, 2 and 4 on the same device are untouched.
  • AP2 forbids Ed25519 on the Checkout JWT. It requires a non deterministic scheme to prevent rainbow table attacks. This codebase is Ed25519 throughout. Any future AP2 artifact has to move to ECDSA. Recorded now so it is not discovered late.

Considered and not adopted

AP2 Python types PRIMARY

The SDK at code/sdk/python/ap2/models/mandate.py still defines IntentMandate, CartContents, CartMandate, PaymentMandateContents and PaymentMandate. That is v0.1 naming. The documentation describes v0.2. The code lags the specification.

There is also no PyPI release. Installation is from a git branch. A dependency on a moving branch of another organisation's repository would make the results unreproducible. This project holds itself to reproducibility. Decision: vendor the schemas and not the classes. Treat AP2 as related work.

Also considered and rejected. W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 with JSON-LD was rejected because AP2 v0.2 is JWT claims shaped. It uses vct and cnf. So SD-JWT style artifacts are the closer fit and they avoid a heavy canonicalisation dependency. RFC 9396 Rich Authorization Requests informed the authority model but was not adopted wholesale. RFC 8785 JCS was not adopted. The project canonical() function is close to JCS but it is not JCS. Number formatting and UTF-8 handling differ. State that deviation in the thesis.

05Methodology: the simulation

The artifact is a simulator with UPI shaped rails. It has a double entry ledger where money moves only through balanced postings. It has a two leg payment with a suspense account in between. It has a transaction state machine that refuses illegal transitions. It has injectable faults and a reconciliation sweep. Simulator specific response codes carry a SIM- prefix so nobody mistakes them for switch responses.

What each experiment does

06Results

Result 1. No UPI primitive can record the authority

One authority. Pay either of two shops, groceries only, up to ₹5,000. Put to each rail.

  CANNOT   upi_reservepay   blocks funds against one named merchant, so an authority
                            for 2 merchants is not expressible
                            and it carries no notion of what was bought
  CANNOT   upi_circle       attaches to a device and not a payee, so "only these two
                            shops" cannot be recorded. the agent could pay anyone
  CANNOT   card_token       moves the money onto a credential the agent controls
                            before the merchant is paid

  No rail can record what the user said.

The control case matters as much as the finding. A single named merchant with no category is what UPI was built for. It rides Reserve Pay perfectly well. So the gap is specific and not a general complaint. It is a missing vocabulary and not a missing check.

Creating an authority that a named rail cannot express now raises RailCannotExpress. Before, it would have silently recorded a narrower authority than the user set. Recording less than the user asked for and reporting success is worse than refusing. The user believes they are protected.

Result 2. Nobody on UPI can enforce it

A delegated payment produces four documents. The authority, the cart, the payment request and the settlement. Check A asks whether the request still matches the cart. It needs the cart and the request. Check B asks whether the cart fits inside the authority. It needs the cart and the authority.

PartyCheck BWhy
agentnoconflicted. it is the party the check exists to constrain
payer PSPnoblind. never sees the authority. it also runs in the agent environment
remitter banknoblind. holds the authority and does the debit, but has never seen a cart
switch at NPCInoblind. sees neither the authority nor the cart
beneficiary banknoblind. sees neither
merchantnoblind to the authority. it also earns more when the amount is higher
merchant processorn/ano such party exists in a UPI payment

Both checks are unenforceable on UPI as it stands

Every party is either blind or conflicted. The two failure modes are reported separately throughout. Only blindness is fixable by changing the rail. Handing the merchant the authority would not make it trustworthy.

One field changes the answer

Give the remitter bank a cart reference. Both checks become enforceable. They land with the one party that is disinterested and already decides whether to debit. That one field difference is the proposal. It is stated as a diff and not as an argument. It is asserted in tests/test_authority.py.

Result 3. The limit protects. The signature does not.

A self consistent inflated order is one whose lines genuinely add to ₹600. It was run past all three signing arrangements. Reproduced through the bench API.

  gate OFF, agreed 118, charged 600, ceiling 1000  → ALLOWED   (UPI today)
  gate ON,  same payment                           → REFUSED

  self consistent 600 order signed by agent        → ALLOWED
  self consistent 600 order signed by merchant     → ALLOWED
  self consistent 600 order signed by both         → ALLOWED
  same order, ceiling dropped to 150               → REFUSED   ← the limit, not the signature

A signature proves who wrote a record. It does not prove the record is true. Once the order is the only surviving evidence of the agreement there is nothing left to compare it against. Requiring both signatures also has a measurable cost. If merchants must sign and none has integrated then every payment stops. That includes the honest ones.

Result 4. The rail can now say who started a debit

Section II C of the brief notes that the rail carries no field saying who or what started a debit. Until this work the simulator reproduced that hole faithfully. Transaction had no agent on it at all. It now does. The value comes from an owner issued credential and is not asserted by the agent.

Result 5. The books survive every failure

Each of seven injectable faults was sent through the bench. The ledger is derived again from the entries afterwards. It is not read from a cached balance.

  (none)               → SETTLED    00    entries=4   healthy=True
  credit_fail          → REVERSED   U31   entries=4   healthy=True
  debit_timeout        → REVERSED   U31   entries=4   healthy=True   recon: {'reversed': 1}
  insufficient_funds   → DECLINED   Z9    entries=0   healthy=True
  beneficiary_down     → REVERSED   U28   entries=4   healthy=True

A timed out debit is neither failed nor successful. The switch does not know. It resolves only by asking the banks what actually posted. That is what the reconciliation sweep does.

Result 6. What the card rail costs, measured

Cards solve the problem UPI cannot express. The agent holds a credential, so the authority does not need to name a shop in advance. The cost is custody, and it is three separate things rather than one.

The same trade appears if you try to make UPI work this way. Name the agent card as the payee on a Reserve Pay block and the block becomes expressible, because there is one payee. What you have recorded is no longer an instruction about shops.

CostMeasured result
Merchant scope stops workingA mandate scoped to Brewhouse funded the card, and the card then paid CloudHost successfully. The same payment made directly was refused with cloudhost@okaxis is not a permitted payee.
Headroom burns on the loadPulling ₹600 for a ₹499 purchase consumed ₹600 of the user cap whether or not the purchase completed. ₹101 stranded on the card.
Float strands invisiblyLoad succeeds, spend fails, and the money sits on the card. The reconciliation sweep does not find it, because nothing is stuck mid transaction. Only a deliberate sweep returns it.
Audit chain severedLoad and spend are separate transactions with separate references. The load record names the card and never the merchant. The link exists only because the application kept it.
Revocation has three targetsKilling the card token, stopping future loads and returning the float are three actions, not one. The float is the one that gets forgotten. A revoke that half worked is worse than one that failed loudly.

The trade, stated plainly

UPI can carry an agent authority today only by making the agent the merchant. That removes merchant scope, burns headroom on the load, strands float and severs the audit chain. It converts a scope problem into a custody problem. It does not remove the problem.

In production this arrangement holds customer funds. In India that needs a PPI or PA licence. SECONDARY That is a funding question and not a simulation question, but it decides who can deploy the design.

07What is not simulated

Stated plainly rather than left for a reviewer to find.

Hardware attestation is a stub

The attestation claim is shaped as a real Entity Attestation Token. The roles come from the RATS architecture. The evidence behind it is fabricated. There is no Intel TDX. There is no AMD SEV-SNP. There is no Secure Enclave quote. This runs on a laptop. A standard format does not make a stub true. So every attestation carries simulated: true and dbgstat: "not-disabled".

A real half step exists and it is free. The macOS Secure Enclave holds a non exportable P-256 key. That makes key theft demonstrable rather than assumed. It is not remote attestation. A verifier still cannot check what code is using the key. It is also P-256 and not the Ed25519 used elsewhere.

  • Real UPI. Reserve Pay and UPI Circle behaviour comes from circular text that has not been read. UNVERIFIED NPCI OC 228 and OC 201-B, and every money ceiling derived from them. The code does not depend on the figures being right. It depends on the shape of what each rail can and cannot say. That shape is better attested.
  • Card network scope enforcement. Mastercard Agentic Tokens validate scope at the network. The check can be modelled. The network cannot.
  • AP2 interoperability. Artifacts matching the schemas can be produced. No counterparty runs the protocol. So acceptance cannot be shown.
  • Dispute economics. Who absorbs a bad agent payment is a liability question with no public data. The simulator can show what evidence exists at the moment of dispute. It cannot say who pays.
  • Prompt injection. No bound can be placed on how susceptible a model is.
  • P3P prior art. CONTESTED Pine Labs P3P runs agentic payments on UPI Reserve Pay with an identity layer called Grantex. Whether it binds a payment to an approved order is still unchecked. The AP2 finding does not replace that check. It remains the highest priority open item.

08What remains

  1. The cross rail receipt. One signed record binding the registration, the authority, the order and both settlement legs. It verifies the amounts agree across all four. It checks every signature from the public key resolver. This closes a gap the project has already measured. tests/test_vcard.py asserts the audit chain is severed because the load names the card and never the merchant.
  2. The dispute path. The three outcome states exist. What is missing is what happens next. Assemble the receipt. Compare the revocation timestamp against settlement. State who is answerable. A payment at 14:06 against a revoke at 14:05.
  3. Exposure through the REST layer. Agents and receipts alongside the existing 23 routes in api.py.
  4. Prompt injection demonstration. Not one of the six components. It is cheap and reproducible. Feed the real LLM planner a poisoned invoice. Let it emit a corrupted intent. Show the deterministic gate refuses it anyway. It demonstrates the claim the architecture rests on. The model is not in the approval path.
  5. Read the circulars. Every UPI figure stays unverified until OC 228 and OC 201-B are read section by section.
